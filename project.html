01 — The Phenomenon

A tone that exists
without a source

When two sustained tones sound simultaneously, the ear generates a third pitch — one that has no physical source in the room. This is the difference tone: a phantom frequency equal to the arithmetic difference between the two sounding pitches.

It is not an electronic effect, not a recording artefact. It is acoustic physics, and it happens inside the listener.

02 — Composing the Phantom

The melody is written.
Computation finds the carriers.

The composer writes a melody — but every note of that melody is a ghost tone. The desired pitch has no direct physical form. It can only exist if two carrier instruments sustain exactly the right frequency pair to produce it.

The mathematics runs in reverse: given a target phantom pitch, derive the two carrier frequencies required to generate it. This is what the software does. The composer hears the melody. The system works out how to make it real.

03 — The Instrument

A synthesiser built
to think in phantom tones

Before writing for acoustic musicians, the logic had to be proven electronically. A software synthesiser — inspired by Buchla 700 architecture — was developed as a compositional sketchpad: an instrument where every note played is a difference tone, generated by two carrier oscillators whose relationship is derived automatically from the intended pitch.

The synthesiser allows rapid exploration of carrier relationships, harmonic spacing, and timbral character without the constraint of real performers. It is the experimental environment in which the compositional method was found.

04 — Polyphony

Four phantom voices.
Eight carrier lines.

The logic extends naturally to polyphony. Four simultaneous ghost tones require four carrier pairs — eight distinct lines, each pair independently tuned to produce its target phantom pitch. The harmony the listener hears is composed entirely of notes that do not physically exist.

05 — Space

Each phantom voice
has a location

The carrier pairs are positioned apart in the performance space — one on each side of the audience. The ghost tone they generate does not come from either musician. It appears in the air between them, at a location determined by the geometry of the room and the position of the listener.

The spatial arrangement is not incidental. It is compositional. Each of the four phantom voices occupies a different position in the room. The audience sits inside the harmony.

06 — The Score

A generative score
derived from phantom melodies

The notation the musicians read is a computational artefact. The composer writes a melody in phantom tones. A MIDI conversion system — developed alongside the synthesiser — derives the carrier pairs for each note and translates them into microtonal instrumental parts, with cent-accurate pitch markings the performers can tune to.

No human calculates these parts by hand. The score is generated. The musicians perform the algorithm's output. The melody appears in the room.
